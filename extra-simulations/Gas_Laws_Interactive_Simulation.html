Pressure–Volume Graph

As volume falls, pressure climbs along an inverse curve — never a straight line.

The relationship

Volume ↓ → gas particles are squeezed into a smaller space → they hit the walls of the syringe more often → Pressure ↑.

This is an inverse relationship: pressure and volume move in opposite directions, at constant temperature.

Boyle's Law & Worked Example

P1V1 = P2V2
P₁ = initial pressure  ·  V₁ = initial volume  ·  P₂ = final pressure  ·  V₂ = final volume  ·  temperature and mass of gas held constant

"For a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature, pressure is inversely proportional to volume."

Volume–Temperature Graph

Volume plotted against temperature in kelvin gives a straight line through 0 K.

The relationship

Temperature ↑ → gas particles gain kinetic energy and move faster → they push the piston outward → Volume ↑.

This is a direct relationship: volume and temperature (in kelvin) rise and fall together, at constant pressure.

Charles' Law & Worked Example

V1/T1 = V2/T2
V₁,V₂ = initial/final volume  ·  T₁,T₂ = initial/final temperature in kelvin  ·  T(K) = T(°C) + 273  ·  pressure and mass of gas held constant

"For a fixed mass of gas at constant pressure, volume is directly proportional to its absolute (kelvin) temperature."

50.0 ÷ 298  =  50.0 ÷ 298  →  0.168 = 0.168 ✓

Rate of Diffusion vs 1/√Molar Mass

Rate of diffusion is directly proportional to 1/√M — a straight line through the origin.

The relationship

Molar mass ↓ → particles are lighter, so for the same kinetic energy they move faster → Rate of diffusion ↑.

This is an inverse-square-root relationship — heavier gases always diffuse more slowly than lighter ones, under the same conditions.

Graham's Law & Worked Example

RA/RB = √(MB/MA)
R = rate of diffusion  ·  M = molar mass  ·  same temperature and pressure for both gases

"The rate of diffusion of a gas is inversely proportional to the square root of its molar mass."

Gas A — Ammonia

MA = 17 g/mol

Gas B — HCl

MB = 36.5 g/mol
RA/RB = √(36.5/17) = 1.47  →  ammonia diffuses 1.47× faster than HCl
